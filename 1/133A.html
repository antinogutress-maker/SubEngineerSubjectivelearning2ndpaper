Three-Point Problem

Plane Tabling Solutions

AUTO-LOOP

The Problem: Locating the exact position of the plane table station (p) on the drawing paper by sighting three well-defined, already plotted points (a, b, c) whose corresponding ground points (A, B, C) are visible.

1. Mechanical (Tracing Paper)

  • Place a translucent tracing paper over the board.
  • Assume a point p' on it and draw rays to ground points A, B, C.
  • Unfasten the tracing paper and slide/rotate it over the drawing until the three drawn rays pass exactly through the plotted points a, b, c on the main sheet.
  • Prick the center point through to the drawing paper to get the true p.

2. Graphical (Bessel's Method)

Constructing the Inscribed Quadrilateral:

  • Orient the table along line ab and sight point C. Draw a ray from a.
  • Orient along ba and sight point C. Draw a ray from b.
  • The intersection of these two rays gives point d.
  • Draw a line connecting c to d. The true station p lies somewhere on this line.
  • Sight A (or B) to draw a final resection ray, intersecting cd at the exact location p.

3. Trial & Error (Lehman's)

Solving the Triangle of Error:

  • Orient the table by eye/compass and draw resection rays from A, B, and C through a, b, and c.
  • If orientation is imperfect, the rays won't meet at a point, but will form a small Triangle of Error.
  • Using Lehman's Rules (looking left/right, proportional distances), estimate a new point p1.
  • Rotate the board slightly to align p1 with a target. Redraw the rays.
  • Repeat until the triangle vanishes into a single point p.
