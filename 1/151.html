1. Gentle Slope

Scroll Down 👇

Widely Spaced

Contours spaced far apart horizontally denote a gentle incline. The terrain rises slowly, requiring a long horizontal distance to climb to the next elevation interval. Notice the wide gaps in the 2D map.

2. Steep Slope

Closely Spaced

Tightly packed contours indicate a steep slope or cliff. Elevation changes rapidly over a very short horizontal distance. The 2D map shows dense lines clustered together.

3. Uniform Slope

Equally Spaced

When the horizontal distance between every contour line is identical, the terrain is a uniform, perfectly flat angled plane. The rate of incline never changes.

4. Uneven Surface

Irregularly Spaced

Wavy, irregular contour lines with varying distances represent undulating or rocky terrain. Natural landscapes mostly consist of these unpredictable bumps and dips.

5. Pond / Depression

Concentric (Decreasing)

Closed concentric loops where elevation values decrease inward represent a depression, crater, or pond. The terrain dips downward into a bowl shape.

6. Hill / Peak

Concentric (Increasing)

Closed loops where elevation values increase inward represent a hill or mountain. The innermost circle is the peak or summit of the landform.

7. Ridge Line (U-Shape)

Convex to Lower Ground

When contours form a U-shape pointing downward toward lower elevation, they define a ridge or spur. It's high ground jutting forward. Water sheds away from the central red ridge axis.

8. Valley (V-Shape)

Convex to Higher Ground

When contours form sharp V-shapes pointing upward toward higher elevation, they define a valley. It's a trough carved into the land, where a stream (blue) naturally flows downward.
