1. Direct Method

Actually Traced on Ground: The instrument (Level) stays fixed while the staff man moves up/down the slope until the exact required staff reading is found.
✓ Highly Accurate | ✓ For Small Works | ❌ Very Slow

2. Method of Cross-Sections

For Route Surveys: Best for roads, railways, and canals. A longitudinal base line is run, and cross-sections are taken perpendicular to it at regular intervals. Spot levels are taken along these cross-lines.
✓ Best for Long, Narrow Strips | ✓ Interpolated Contours

3. Method of Squares

Grid Method: The area is divided into a grid of squares. Elevations are observed exactly at the corners (nodes) of the squares. Contours are then drawn by interpolating between the corner values.
✓ Best for Flat / Gently Sloping Ground | ✓ Easy to Plot

4. Tacheometric Method

Radial Method: An optical transit (Tacheometer) is set up centrally. Radial lines are observed at specific angles, and spot levels/distances are calculated optically without physical taping.
✓ Best for Hilly / Rough / Inaccessible Terrain
