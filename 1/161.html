Setting Out

Simplified Study Guide for High Marks

Setting Out Buildings

"The process of establishing the center lines of walls and excavation trenches on the ground based on the structural plan."

All Methods for Small Buildings:

  • Circumference Method: Setting out the outer boundary of the building.
  • Center-Line Method: The standard engineering approach for foundations.
  • 3-4-5 Method: A geometric tool used within any layout to check for squareness.

⭐ Detail Focus: The Center-Line Method

This is the most reliable method because it directly relates to the column and wall axes. It uses string lines to represent the center of every structural element.

Step 1: Establishment of Baseline
A main baseline is established parallel to a permanent feature (like a road). The first wall center is located from this line.
Step 2: Marking Main Corners
The four main corners of the building are located using theodolite or tape (via 3-4-5 rule) to ensure they are perfectly rectangular.
Step 3: Fixing Profile Boards (Batter Boards)
Wooden boards are driven into the ground approx. 2m outside the building area. This ensures they aren't disturbed during excavation.
Step 4: Stretching String Lines
Nails are driven into the profile boards. String (nylon twine) is stretched between nails to represent the center lines of all walls.
Step 5: Transferring to Ground
Plumb bobs are dropped from the string intersections to mark the center line on the soil. Trench widths are then measured out from these center lines.
HIGH-MARK TIP: Mention that the "Height" or "Level" of the profile boards is usually set to the Plinth Level using a Leveling instrument. This allows the boards to serve as a reference for both position AND depth.

Setting Out Simple Circular Curves

"Connecting two straight paths (tangents) with a single circular arc of constant radius (R)."

Methods Categorized:

1. Linear Methods (Tape only):

  • Offsets from Long Chord
  • Successive Bisection of Arcs
  • Offsets from Tangents

2. Angular Methods (Instruments):

  • Rankine’s Method (Single Theodolite)
  • Two-Theodolite Method (No Tape needed)
  • Tacheometric Method

⭐ Detail Focus: Rankine's Method (Deflection Angles)

This is the standard high-accuracy method used in actual field work and the most common exam question.

The Core Formula:

δ = (1718.9 × c) / R (minutes)

δ = Deflection angle, c = Chord length, R = Radius

Step 1: Set up theodolite at the Tangent Point (T₁).
Step 2: Sight the point of intersection (V) with a reading of 0°0'0".
Step 3: Calculate the first deflection angle (δ₁) for the first chord.
Step 4: Turn the theodolite to angle δ₁ and measure distance 'c' from T₁ to locate the first point.
Step 5: Repeat for subsequent points using cumulative angles.
EXAM TIP: Always draw a small sketch of a curve showing T₁, T₂, R, and the deflection angle Δ. This easily adds 2-3 extra marks!
MNEMONIC R-A-T: Rankine = Angular + Tape.
