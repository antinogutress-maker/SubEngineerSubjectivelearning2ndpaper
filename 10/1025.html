10.2.5 Super Elevation

In order to counteract the effect of centrifugal force and to reduce the tendency of vehicle to overturn or skid, the outer edge of pavement is raised with respect to inner edge thus providing a transverse slope throughout the length of horizontal curve. This transverse inclination to the pavement surface is known as super elevation or cant or banking.

Analysis of super elevation

The forces acting on the vehicle moving on a circular curve of radius R at speed v m/s as shown in figure. The forces are;

  1. Centrifugal force (P) acting horizontally outward through cg
  2. Weight of vehicle (W) acting vertically downward through cg
  3. Frictional force developed between wheels and pavement

At equilibrium condition

$$ \begin{aligned} P \cos \theta &= W \sin \theta + F_A + F_B = W \sin \theta + f(R_A + R_B) \\[8pt] P \cos \theta &= W \sin \theta + P f \sin \theta + W f \cos \theta \\[8pt] P(\cos \theta - f \sin \theta) &= W(\sin \theta + f \cos \theta) \\[8pt] \frac{P}{W} &= \frac{\sin \theta + f \cos \theta}{\cos \theta - f \sin \theta} = \frac{\tan \theta + f}{1 - f \tan \theta} \\[12pt] \frac{P}{W} &= \frac{e + f}{1} = e + f \quad [\because 1 - f \sin \theta \simeq 1, f = 0.15, e = 0.07] \\[12pt] \therefore e + f &= \frac{P}{W} \\[8pt] e + f &= \frac{v^2}{gR} \quad [v = m/s] \\[8pt] e + f &= \frac{V^2}{127R} \quad [V = kmph] \end{aligned} $$
