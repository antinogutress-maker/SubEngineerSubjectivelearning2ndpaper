Road Gradients

Highway Engineering

AUTO-LOOP

1. Ruling (Design) Gradient

The Ideal Slope: The maximum gradient within which the designer attempts to align the road. Vehicles can comfortably cruise up this slope in top gear without losing speed.

2. Limiting Gradient

The Cost-Saver: Steeper than ruling gradient. Used when topography forces it, to save massive construction costs. Vehicles will lose speed and drivers must shift to a lower gear.

3. Exceptional Gradient

The Extreme Case: Very steep and strictly limited to extremely short stretches (e.g., < 100m). Used only in unavoidable situations. Causes severe engine strain.

4. Minimum Gradient

For Drainage: A totally flat road (0%) causes rainwater to pond on the surface. A very mild minimum gradient (e.g., 1 in 200) is provided purely to drain rainwater off the road.

5. Floating Gradient

The Equilibrium Slope: A specific descending gradient where the downward pull of gravity exactly balances the rolling friction. A vehicle descends at a constant speed without braking or accelerating.
