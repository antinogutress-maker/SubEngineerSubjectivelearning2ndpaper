Surface Drainage Methods

Surface drainage is the removal of rainwater from the pavement surface to prevent loss of friction and structural damage.

  • 1. Camber (Cross Slope): The convexity provided to the road. It rapidly diverts water from the crown (center) to the edges.
  • 2. Longitudinal Drains: Open side drains running parallel to the road. They collect the runoff from the camber and safely channel it away to natural watercourses.

Slide Camber to 0% to observe hazardous water ponding.

Sub-Surface Drainage

Sub-surface drainage prevents groundwater from softening the subgrade. Capillary action can draw water up from the water table, especially if it is trapped by an impermeable layer.

  • 1. Impermeable Stratum: A hard clay or rock layer that traps water above it, creating a "perched" water table.
  • 2. Longitudinal Trench Drains: Deep trenches filled with filter material and perforated pipes, dug down to the impermeable layer to intercept and lower the water table (Drawdown Curve).

Activate Trench Drains to observe the drawdown curve protect the subgrade.
