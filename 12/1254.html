Bar Chart (Gantt)

Construction Management

SYNCED

1. The Anatomy

Time vs Activity: Introduced by Henry Gantt. The X-axis represents Time (Days/Weeks), and the Y-axis lists the Activities. The length of the bar directly visually shows the duration of that specific job.

2. Phase I: Substructure

Months 1-2: The foundation of the house. The chart schedules sequential tasks: First, Excavation and trenching, followed directly by Concrete pouring and Foundation laying below ground level.

3. Phase II: Superstructure

Months 3-4: Above-ground construction. Notice how the "Roofing" activity bar cannot begin until the "Brickwork/Walls" activity bar is completely finished.

4. Phase III: Finishing

Months 5-6: Final touches. Bar charts excel at showing overlapping activities—like Plastering, Painting, and Installing Windows happening concurrently to save time.

5. The Fatal Limitation

No Interdependencies: The major flaw of a Bar Chart is that it does not show which activities depend on others. If Activity A is delayed, the chart doesn't automatically show how it impacts Activity B (This led to the creation of CPM/PERT networks).

6. Complete Summary

Advantages

  • Simple to read & draw.
  • Clearly shows start/end dates.
  • Excellent for small projects.
  • Great visual tool for tracking overall progress.

Disadvantages

  • No dependencies shown.
  • Lacks Critical Path identification.
  • Hard to update dynamically.
  • Unsuitable for large, complex projects.
