Schmidt Rebound Hammer Test

Non-Destructive Testing (NDT) of Concrete

❓ Why is it done?

During construction, concrete samples (cubes or cylinders) are tested in a lab. If the lab cubes fail, or if we need to check the structural integrity of an old building, we cannot simply destroy the existing columns or slabs to test them.

We use Non-Destructive Testing (NDT) to estimate the strength of the concrete in-situ (on site) without causing any damage to the structure.

⚙️ How does it work?

  • Energy Transfer: The hammer contains a spring-loaded mass. When pushed against the wall, the mass strikes the plunger with a fixed amount of energy.
  • Hard vs. Weak: If the concrete is weak/porous, it absorbs the energy (crushing slightly on a microscopic level), causing a low bounce back. If the concrete is hard/dense, it reflects the energy, causing a high bounce.
  • The bounce distance is recorded as the Rebound Number (R), which correlates to the Compressive Strength in MPa.
