Softening Point of Bitumen

Ring and Ball Test (Thermoplastic Behavior Analysis)

☀️ Why We Do It (The Field Reality)

Bitumen is thermoplastic; it doesn't melt instantly like ice, it gradually softens as temperature rises.

During summer in the Terai region, road surface temperatures can exceed 55°C. If bitumen softens at a lower temperature (e.g., 45°C), heavy trucks will push the aggregates around, causing deep ruts (tire tracks) and "bleeding" (liquid bitumen rising to the surface).

🔬 Apparatus & Procedure

  • Apparatus: Brass frame, tapered brass rings, two 3.5g steel balls, liquid bath, thermometer, and a magnetic stirrer.
  • Assembly: Bitumen is poured flush into the rings. A steel ball is placed perfectly in the center.
  • Heating (Crucial): The strict rule is heating the water at exactly 5°C per minute. Stirring ensures uniform heat.
The "Experienced" Interview Answer:
"Sir, the Softening Point is the exact temperature recorded at the moment the sagging bitumen, pushed by the steel ball, touches the bottom metal plate exactly 25.4 mm below the ring."
