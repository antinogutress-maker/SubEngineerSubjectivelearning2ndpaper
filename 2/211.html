Stone Masonry Rules

Construction Materials

AUTO-LOOP

1. Natural Bed (Cleavage)

Stones must be laid so that the pressure/load acts perpendicular (90°) to their natural bed (cleavage plane). If laid parallel to the bed, the stone will split or delaminate under pressure.

2. Break the Joints

Vertical joints must not be continuous. They must be staggered (broken) in successive courses. Continuous vertical joints act as a weak plane and will cause the wall to crack and fail.

3. Through Stones (Headers)

A thick wall has two faces (inner and outer). Through stones (or headers) are long stones laid across the full thickness of the wall at regular intervals to tie the two faces together and prevent bulging.

4. Wetting & Curing

Stones must be wetted before laying so they don't absorb moisture from the mortar (which weakens it). After construction, the masonry must be kept wet (cured) for 2 to 3 weeks for proper strength.

5. Laid on Broad Face

Stones should be properly dressed and laid on their broadest flat face. A stone laid on its narrow edge acts like a pivot, making the wall highly unstable and prone to tipping.

6. Hearting (Filling Voids)

The inner core (hearting) of a thick wall must be properly packed with mortar and stone chips (spalls). Leaving hollow voids weakens the structure and allows water infiltration.

7. Perpendicularity Check

The verticality of the wall faces must be strictly checked using a plumb bob. Crooked walls induce eccentric loading, leading to severe instability and structural collapse.
