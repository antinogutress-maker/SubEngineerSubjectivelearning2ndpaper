Understanding Fluid Energies & Grade Lines

According to Bernoulli's equation, the total energy of an incompressible fluid flowing in a pipe consists of three independent heads (energies per unit weight):

  • 1. Potential Head (Z): Energy due to the elevation of the fluid above a reference datum line.
  • 2. Pressure Head (P / \gamma): Energy stored in the fluid due to static pressure. It represents the height a column of water would rise in a physical tube.
  • 3. Kinetic or Velocity Head (v^2 / 2g): Kinetic energy due to the bulk motion of the fluid.

The Grade Lines Explained

Energy Grade Line (EGL)

Represents the Total Head available in the fluid.

EGL = Z + \frac{P}{\gamma} + \frac{v^2}{2g}

In a real fluid, the EGL always drops in the direction of flow due to friction losses (h_L).

Hydraulic Grade Line (HGL)

Represents the Piezometric Head (Elevation + Pressure).

HGL = Z + \frac{P}{\gamma} = EGL - \frac{v^2}{2g}

The HGL lies exactly below the EGL by a distance equal to the Kinetic Head. If the pipe narrows, velocity increases, so the HGL dips down significantly!
