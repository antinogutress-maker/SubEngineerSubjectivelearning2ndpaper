Pipe vs Channel Flow

Fluid Mechanics / Hydraulics

AUTO-LOOP

1. Driving Force

Pressure vs. Gravity: Pipe flow is strictly driven by a pressure difference (like a pump pushing water). Open channel flow is driven entirely by the component of gravity acting along the slope of the bed.

2. Free Surface (Atmosphere)

Enclosed vs. Exposed: A pipe flows 100% full under pressure with no free surface. An open channel has a free surface that is directly exposed to atmospheric pressure.

3. Hydraulic Grade Line (HGL)

Above vs. On the Surface: In pipe flow, the HGL (Pressure Head + Elevation) generally lies above the pipe. In open channel flow, the HGL coincides exactly with the free water surface.

4. Pressure Distribution

Radial vs. Hydrostatic: Pipe flow has intense radial pressure pushing outwards on the pipe walls. Open channel flow has a hydrostatic pressure distribution (zero gauge pressure at the top, max at the bed).

5. Velocity Profile

Center vs. Sub-Surface: Due to wall friction, max velocity in a pipe is exactly at the center. In a channel, air resistance at the top pushes the max velocity slightly below the free surface.
