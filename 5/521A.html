Loksewa Exam Guide
Terzaghi's Principle of Effective Stress

Marks: 5

1. Definition (1.5 Marks)

According to Karl Terzaghi, the total vertical stress applied to a saturated soil mass is carried in two parts: by the solid soil grains and by the water in the voids. "All measurable effects of a change of stress, such as compression and a change of shearing resistance, are exclusively due to changes in effective stress."

2. Equation (1.5 Marks)

\( \sigma' = \sigma - u \)
  • \( \sigma \) (Total Stress): Pressure due to weight of soil, water, and external loads.
  • \( u \) (Pore Pressure): Hydrostatic pressure of water in voids. Acts in all directions (neutral stress).
  • \( \sigma' \) (Effective Stress): Actual intergranular contact pressure.

3. Mechanism (1 Mark)

Effective stress (\( \sigma' \)) is the sum of normal forces at grain contact points per unit area. When water table rises, buoyant pore pressure (\( u \)) increases, pushing particles apart and reducing contact friction (\( \sigma' \)).

4. Engineering Significance (1 Mark)

  • Shear Strength: Soil strength (\( \tau = c' + \sigma' \tan \phi' \)) depends entirely on effective stress. A drop in \( \sigma' \) weakens soil (e.g., floods).
  • Settlement: Consolidation only happens when effective stress increases (\( \Delta\sigma' > 0 \)).
