Explain Terzaghi's Principle of Effective Stress (5 Marks)

1. Definition & Core Concept (1.5 Marks)

According to Karl Terzaghi (1936), the total vertical stress applied to a saturated soil mass is carried in two parts: by the solid soil skeleton (grains) and by the water occupying the void spaces.
"All measurable effects of a change of stress, such as compression, distortion, and a change of shearing resistance, are exclusively due to changes in the effective stress."

2. Mathematical Formulation (1.5 Marks)

\( \sigma' = \sigma - u \)
  • Total Stress (\( \sigma \)): The total pressure on a horizontal plane due to the weight of soil, water, and any external surcharge (e.g., buildings) above it.
  • Pore Water Pressure (\( u \)): The hydrostatic pressure exerted by water in the voids. It acts equally in all directions (neutral stress) and does not press soil grains together.
  • Effective Stress (\( \sigma' \)): The actual intergranular contact pressure between soil particles. It cannot be measured physically; it must be calculated.

3. Physical Mechanism (1 Mark)

As visualized in the interactive microscopic view, effective stress (\( \sigma' \)) is the sum of the vertical components of normal forces developed at the grain-to-grain contact points, divided by the total area. When the water table rises, the buoyant pore pressure (\( u \)) increases, physically pushing the particles apart and reducing the contact friction (\( \sigma' \)).

4. Practical Engineering Significance (1 Mark)

  • Bearing Capacity & Shear Strength: Soil fails in shear. According to Coulomb's equation (\( \tau = c' + \sigma' \tan \phi' \)), shear strength depends entirely on effective stress. A drop in \( \sigma' \) (e.g., during a flood) drastically weakens the soil, potentially causing foundation failure.
  • Settlement Analysis: Squeezing water out of voids transfers load to the soil skeleton. Consolidation and settlement only happen when effective stress increases (\( \Delta\sigma' > 0 \)).
