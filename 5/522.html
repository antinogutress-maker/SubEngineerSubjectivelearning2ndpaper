Darcy's Law (Theory & Explanation - 5 Marks)

"For laminar flow conditions in a saturated soil mass, the macroscopic rate of flow (discharge velocity) is directly proportional to the hydraulic gradient."

Mathematical Expression:

The law is mathematically expressed as:

v = k · i      or      Q = k · i · A

Where:

  • Q = Total rate of flow or Discharge (Volume/time, e.g., cm³/s)
  • v = Discharge velocity or superficial velocity (Length/time, e.g., cm/s). Note: This is the velocity assuming water flows through the entire cross-section, not just the pore spaces.
  • k = Coefficient of Permeability or Hydraulic Conductivity (Length/time, e.g., cm/s). It is a property of both the porous medium (soil) and the fluid.
  • i = Hydraulic Gradient (Dimensionless). Defined as the head loss (Δh) per unit length (L) of flow path: i = Δh / L.
  • A = Total cross-sectional area of the soil mass perpendicular to the direction of flow (Area, e.g., cm²).

Limitations and Validity:

Darcy's Law is fundamentally valid only for laminar flow. In soils, this condition is generally satisfied when the Reynolds number (R_e) is less than 1. This holds true for almost all naturally occurring soils (clays, silts, sands) under typical hydraulic gradients. However, in very coarse gravels or rock fill subjected to high hydraulic gradients, the flow may become turbulent, and Darcy's law becomes invalid.
