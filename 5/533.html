Relation Between Dry Density & Moisture Content

Loksewa Exam: 5 Marks

1. Introduction to Compaction (1 Mark):
Compaction is the artificial process of increasing the density of soil by mechanically forcing soil particles closer together, expelling air from the voids. The resulting dry density (γ_d) heavily depends on the water content (w) at which the soil is compacted.

2. The Ascending Limb - Lubrication Theory (1.5 Marks):
Initially, when the soil is dry, there is high frictional resistance between particles, preventing dense packing. As water is added (w increases), it acts as a lubricant around the soil grains. Under the same compactive effort, particles easily slide past one another into a denser configuration, resulting in an increase in dry density (γ_d).

3. The Peak - OMC and MDD (1 Mark):
The dry density continues to increase with added water until it reaches a maximum value.

  • Maximum Dry Density (MDD): The peak density achievable for a given compactive effort.
  • Optimum Moisture Content (OMC): The specific water content at which MDD is achieved. At this point, the soil particles are packed as closely as physically possible for that compactive energy.

4. The Descending Limb - Displacement/Swelling (1.5 Marks):
If water is added beyond the OMC, the water begins to occupy the space that could have been filled by solid soil particles. Because water is incompressible and lighter than soil solids, it pushes the soil grains apart. This causes a decrease in dry density (γ_d), forming the downward curve.

Note on Zero Air Voids (ZAV) Line: The theoretical maximum density where 100% of the voids are filled with water (no air).
γd(max) = (Gs · γw) / (1 + w · Gs). The actual compaction curve never intersects the ZAV line because it is practically impossible to expel 100% of the entrapped air by mechanical compaction alone.
