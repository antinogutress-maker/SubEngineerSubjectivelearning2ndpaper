Mohr-Coulomb Failure Theory

Loksewa Format: 5 Marks

1. Basic Concept & Statement (1 Mark):

The Mohr-Coulomb failure theory states that failure in a soil mass occurs not due to maximum normal stress, but due to a critical combination of normal stress and shear stress on a particular plane. Failure occurs when the shear stress reaches a limiting value (shear strength) governed by the material's cohesion and friction.

2. The Failure Equation (1 Mark):

τ_f = c + σ \tan(\phi)

3. Components of Shear Strength (1.5 Marks):

  • τ_f = Shear strength of the soil at failure.
  • c = Cohesion: The inherent intermolecular bond between soil particles, independent of normal stress (dominant in clays).
  • σ = Total Normal Stress acting on the failure plane. (Note: In terms of effective stress, it is written as τ_f = c' + σ' \tan(\phi')).
  • φ = Angle of Internal Friction: Resistance due to the interlocking and rubbing of soil grains (dominant in sands).

4. Graphical Representation & Failure Plane (1.5 Marks):

  • The equation τ_f = c + σ \tan(\phi) represents a straight line on the Mohr diagram, called the Failure Envelope.
  • A soil element is safe if its Mohr's circle lies completely below the failure envelope.
  • Failure occurs when the Mohr's circle expands and just touches (is tangent to) the envelope.
  • The angle of the failure plane (slip plane) with the major principal plane is theoretically given by: θ = 45° + \phi/2.
