Terzaghi's Bearing Capacity Theory

Loksewa Exam: 10 Marks

1. Introduction (1 Mark):
Terzaghi (1943) presented the first comprehensive theory for evaluating the ultimate bearing capacity of shallow foundations. He defined a shallow foundation as one where the depth of the footing (D_f) is less than or equal to its width (B).

2. Key Assumptions (2 Marks):

  • The footing is continuous (strip footing, L \gg B).
  • The base of the footing is rough, preventing soil from sliding along the base.
  • The soil fails in General Shear Failure.
  • The soil above the base of the footing (D_f) is assumed to have no shear strength and acts purely as a surcharge load (q = \gamma \cdot D_f).

3. Failure Mechanism / Zones (2 Marks):
Terzaghi divided the soil mass below the footing into three distinct zones at the point of failure:

  • Zone I (Elastic Wedge): A triangular wedge immediately beneath the footing. Due to base friction, this soil moves downward perfectly rigidly with the footing.
  • Zone II (Radial Shear Zone): Zones radiating from the footing edges. The failure boundaries here are logarithmic spirals.
  • Zone III (Rankine Passive Zone): Triangular zones outside Zone II that are pushed upwards and outwards by the expanding radial zones.

4. Ultimate Bearing Capacity Equation (2 Marks):
Based on equilibrium of the elastic wedge, the ultimate bearing capacity (q_u) for a strip footing is given by:

q_u = c' N_c + q N_q + 0.5 \gamma B N_\gamma
Where:
c' = Effective cohesion of soil.
q = Effective surcharge at the base level (\gamma \cdot D_f).
B = Width of the footing.
N_c, N_q, N_\gamma = Terzaghi's bearing capacity factors, which are functions of the angle of internal friction (\phi) only.

5. Modifications for Footing Shape (1 Mark):
The equation is modified via empirical shape factors for different footing geometries:

  • Square Footing: q_u = 1.3 c' N_c + q N_q + 0.4 \gamma B N_\gamma
  • Circular Footing: q_u = 1.3 c' N_c + q N_q + 0.3 \gamma B N_\gamma

6. Effect of Water Table (1 Mark):
The presence of groundwater significantly reduces bearing capacity because it reduces the effective unit weight (\gamma') of the soil. If the water table is above the footing base, the surcharge term uses submerged unit weight. If it is within depth B below the footing, the wedge term uses an interpolated effective unit weight.

7. Allowable Bearing Capacity (1 Mark):
For design, engineers apply a Factor of Safety (typically FOS = 3) to prevent catastrophic failure:
q_{all} = q_u / FOS or specifically for net pressure: q_{net,all} = (q_u - \gamma D_f) / FOS.
