Analysis of Reinforced Concrete Beams (Limit State Method)

The structural behavior and analysis of Singly and Doubly reinforced concrete beam sections rely on the principles of equilibrium, strain compatibility, and constitutive material laws. The fundamental assumption is that plane sections remain plane before and after bending, resulting in a linear strain profile.

1. Singly Reinforced Section

A singly reinforced beam relies entirely on the compressive strength of the concrete in the top zone and the tensile strength of the steel reinforcing bars (Ast) in the bottom zone. The concrete in tension is completely ignored.

  • Strain Profile: Maximum compressive strain in concrete at failure is fixed at 0.0035. The strain in steel is εst.
  • Stress Block: The concrete stress block is parabolic-rectangular. The maximum stress is 0.45fck. The total compressive force acts at a distance of 0.42xu from the top fiber.
  • Equilibrium: Total Compression (C) = Total Tension (T).
    0.36 fck b xu = 0.87 fy Ast
  • Moment of Resistance (Mu): Mu = T × Lever Arm = 0.87 fy Ast (d - 0.42xu)

2. Doubly Reinforced Section

When the applied bending moment exceeds the limiting moment capacity of a singly reinforced section (Mu,lim), and cross-sectional dimensions (b, D) are restricted, steel reinforcement is added to the compression zone (Asc). This prevents brittle primary compression failure.

  • Compatibility: The strain in the compression steel (εsc) depends on its depth (d') relative to the neutral axis (xu). By similar triangles: εsc = 0.0035 [1 - (d' / xu)].
  • Equilibrium: The total compressive force is shared between concrete (Cc) and compression steel (Cs).
    Cc + Cs = T
    0.36 fck b xu + (fsc - fcc) Asc = 0.87 fy Ast
  • Moment of Resistance: Mu = Mu,lim + fsc Asc (d - d'). The additional tension steel balances the compression steel.
