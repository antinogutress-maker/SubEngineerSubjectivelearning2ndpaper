Types of Foundations

A visual encyclopedia of Shallow and Deep foundations. Designed with structural diagrams and color-coded keywords for rapid memorization and Loksewa exam preparation.

Shallow Foundations

Depth is generally less than or equal to Width (D ≤ B)

1. Isolated / Pad

Isolated (Pad) Footing

Definition: The most common type of foundation. It consists of a single pad (square, rectangular, or circular) provided under a single individual column to distribute its load safely to the soil.

Main Suitability:

  • When columns are lightly loaded and spaced widely apart.
  • When the safe bearing capacity of soil is high at a very shallow depth.
2. Combined

Combined Footing

Definition: A single continuous footing (usually rectangular or trapezoidal) that supports two or more adjacent columns.

Main Suitability:

  • When columns are spaced very closely, causing their individual isolated footings to overlap.
  • When an exterior column is constrained by a property line, restricting symmetric footing growth.
3. Strap / Cantilever

Strap (Cantilever) Footing

Definition: Consists of two separate isolated footings connected by a structural member called a rigid strap beam. The beam transfers bending moments but does not bear on the soil.

Main Suitability:

  • When an exterior column is on a property line, making its footing highly eccentric.
  • When the distance between the two columns is too large to economically construct a combined footing.
4. Mat / Raft

Mat / Raft Foundation

Definition: A large, massive, continuous thick concrete slab covering the entire footprint of the building, supporting all columns and walls.

Main Suitability:

  • When the soil has very low bearing capacity, requiring a massive area to distribute heavy loads.
  • To prevent differential settlement where individual footings would cover more than 50% of the building area.

Deep Foundations

Depth is significantly greater than Width (D ≫ B)

1. Pile Foundation

Pile Foundation

Definition: Slender structural members driven or cast deep into the ground. They transfer loads via End-Bearing (resting on hard strata) or Skin Friction (friction along the pile surface).

Main Suitability:

  • When topsoil is highly compressible or very weak, and hard strata is deep.
  • When the structure is subjected to heavy uplift or lateral forces (e.g., tall towers, offshore structures).
2. Pier Foundation

Pier Foundation

Definition: A vertical, large-diameter cylindrical column (often belled at the bottom) cast-in-situ by excavating a deep shaft, transferring massive loads to stiff strata.

Main Suitability:

  • To transfer exceedingly heavy axial loads directly to underlying bedrock.
  • When top layers contain boulders or stiff clays where driving piles is impossible or causes severe damage.
3. Caisson / Well

Caisson (Well) Foundation

Definition: A watertight retaining structure (box or cylinder) sunk into the ground or water body, then filled with concrete. Widely known as a Well Foundation in bridges.

Main Suitability:

  • For constructing bridge piers over rivers, lakes, or oceans where severe scouring occurs.
  • For massive structures needing placement below water level where dry working conditions are required during excavation.

Memorization Tip: Shallow foundations spread loads horizontally near the surface. Deep foundations transfer loads vertically down to stronger strata.
