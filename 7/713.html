Types of Shoring

1. Raking Shoring (Inclined)

Mechanism: Inclined timber or steel members (rakers) support an unsafe wall. The lateral forces are safely transmitted down to the ground.

  • Used when a wall is bulging or leaning outward.
  • Angle of raker is usually between 45° to 75°.
  • Red arrows indicate the continuous load path being diverted to the solid ground.

2. Flying Shoring (Horizontal)

Mechanism: Horizontal supports placed between two parallel walls. They counteract the inward forces of both buildings against each other.

  • Used when an intermediate building is demolished.
  • Does not touch the ground (keeps the working area clear).
  • Maximum effective span is usually around 9 to 12 meters.

3. Dead Shoring (Vertical)

Mechanism: Vertical props (dead shores) support a horizontal beam (needle) inserted through a hole in the wall to temporarily carry the upper load.

  • Used when the lower part of a wall is being removed or rebuilt.
  • Allows creation of large openings (doors/windows) in load-bearing walls.
  • Transfers the entire upper dead weight to the ground.

Types of Dewatering

Open Sump Pumping

Water seeps into the excavation, flows to a localized pit (sump), and is pumped out directly.

Best for: Shallow depths, coarse soils.

Well Point System

Series of small pipes driven around the pit connected to a header pump, lowering the water table creating a "Cone of Depression".

Best for: Sandy soils, lowering table ~6m.

Deep Well System

Large diameter wells with submersible pumps placed deep below the excavation level. Creates a massive drawdown curve.

Best for: Very deep excavations (>6m), large areas.

Electro-Osmosis

DC current is passed between an Anode (+) and Cathode (-). Water in fine clay physically migrates to the negative well to be pumped.

Best for: Highly cohesive soils (Silts, dense Clays).
