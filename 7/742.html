Well Graded Aggregates

Observation: The grading curve forms a smooth, continuous "S" shape. The physical simulation shows large rocks, with medium rocks filling the large gaps, and fine sands filling the remaining tiny gaps.

Characteristics & Engineering Impact:

  • Contains particles of all standard sizes in appropriate proportions.
  • Results in minimum void space between particles, creating a highly dense mass.
  • Requires less cement paste to fill the voids, making the concrete mix highly economical.
  • Yields concrete that is highly workable and attains maximum compressive strength.
Poorly Graded (Uniform) Aggregates

Observation: The grading curve is very steep, almost a vertical line, indicating particles are concentrated around one specific size. The physical simulation shows large, visible voids between the identical stones.

Characteristics & Engineering Impact:

  • A majority of the particles are of the exact same size (Uniformly graded).
  • Because there are no smaller particles to fit in the gaps, it results in a very high void ratio.
  • Requires a large amount of cement paste to fill these empty voids, making the mix highly uneconomical.
  • If cement paste is insufficient, it leads to porous, weak, and permeable concrete.
Gap Graded Aggregates

Observation: The grading curve has a distinctly flat, horizontal section. The physical simulation shows large rocks and very fine dust, but completely lacks any medium-sized intermediate stones.

Characteristics & Engineering Impact:

  • One or more intermediate sizes are completely missing from the sample.
  • Can occur naturally or be intentionally engineered for specific architectural finishes (e.g., exposed aggregate concrete).
  • Prone to segregation during vibration because the fine mortar can easily separate from the large coarse aggregate.
  • Requires careful mix design to achieve adequate strength compared to well-graded mixes.
