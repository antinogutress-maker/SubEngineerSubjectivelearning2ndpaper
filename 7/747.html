Types of Formwork (Shuttering)

1. Timber Formwork

Traditional formwork made of wooden planks and runners. It is easy to cut and assemble on-site.

  • Leaves a wood grain texture on the concrete.
  • Can absorb water from concrete if not oiled.
  • Low initial cost but limited reusability.

2. Steel Formwork

Constructed from steel plates stiffened by angles. Highly durable and rigid.

  • Produces a perfectly smooth, unblemished finish.
  • Zero water absorption (completely impermeable).
  • High initial cost, but can be reused hundreds of times.

3. Plywood Formwork

Large manufactured wooden sheets backed by a timber frame.

  • Covers large areas quickly with fewer joints.
  • Provides a smoother finish than standard timber.
  • Often resin-coated to prevent water absorption.

Methods of Curing

1. Water Curing Methods

A. Ponding

Small clay/sand bounds are built across a flat slab, and water is filled into these "ponds".

Best for: Horizontal slabs, pavements, floors.

B. Immersion

The concrete element is completely submerged in a tank of curing water.

Best for: Lab test cubes, small precast elements.

C. Spraying / Fogging

Water is continuously or periodically sprayed over the concrete surface to keep it continually damp.

Best for: Vertical columns, retaining walls.

2. Advanced & Alternative Curing

Membrane / Chemical Curing

A liquid curing compound is sprayed. It dries to form an impermeable film that traps internal moisture.

Best for: Areas with water scarcity, large highways.

Steam Curing

Concrete is placed in a chamber filled with hot steam. The heat and moisture accelerate hydration massively.

Best for: Pre-cast concrete factories (rapid demolding).

Wet Coverings (Gunny Bags)

Concrete is wrapped in moisture-retaining fabrics like burlap or gunny bags, which are kept constantly wet.

Best for: Columns, complex shapes on standard sites.
