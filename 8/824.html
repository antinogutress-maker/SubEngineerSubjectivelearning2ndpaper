1. Depression Spring

A type of Gravity Spring. Formed when the physical ground surface sharply dips or drops below the local water table of an unconfined aquifer.

Water simply oozes out into the valley because the topography intersects the saturated zone. Often forms natural lakes or streams.

2. Contact Spring

A type of Gravity Spring. Formed when a highly permeable layer (like sand/gravel) sits directly on top of an impermeable layer (like solid rock or clay).

Water percolates downward, hits the bedrock block, travels horizontally along the contact line, and emerges at a hillside cut.

3. Artesian Spring

Pressure-Driven: Water is trapped in a confined aquifer sandwiched between two impermeable layers. The recharge zone is at a much higher elevation.

Because the piezometric (pressure) head is above ground level, if a natural fissure/fault cracks the top layer, water shoots up like a fountain.

4. Shallow vs. Deep Wells

Defined by the Aquifer tapped, NOT physical depth.

  • Shallow Well: Taps the top unconfined aquifer (above the first impervious layer MOTTUM). Yield varies, dries up easily.
  • Deep Well: Drilled through the impervious layer into a confined aquifer. High, constant yield due to pressure and vast catchment area.
