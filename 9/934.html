Weir & Barrage Siting

Irrigation Engineering

AUTO-LOOP

1. Straight Reach

The river should be straight upstream and downstream of the site. A meandering/curved river causes uneven flow, leading to heavy scouring on the outer bank and silt deposition on the inner bank.

2. Narrow Channel

The river width should be as narrow as possible. A narrower cross-section dramatically reduces the length of the weir, saving massive amounts of concrete and minimizing overall construction costs.

3. High, Stable Banks

The banks must be well-defined, high, and stable. A weir artificially raises the water level (Afflux). If the banks are low, the raised water will overtop (flank) the banks and flood surrounding areas.

4. Strong Foundation Bed

A solid rock or highly compacted soil bed is required. It provides high bearing capacity to support the massive concrete structure and prevents hazardous sub-surface seepage (piping failure).

Needs of a Weir / Barrage

  • 1

    To raise the water level (create afflux) so that water can easily flow into the off-taking irrigation canal.

  • 2

    To regulate the intake of water into the canal, ensuring a consistent supply regardless of river fluctuations.

  • 3

    To control silt entry into the canal by using under-sluices to flush heavy sediment downstream.

  • 4

    To temporarily store water to meet immediate municipal, agricultural, or industrial demands during dry spells.

  • 5

    To safely pass high flood discharges downstream without causing structural damage to the headworks.
